Recording a Quitclaim Deed in Anaheim

To transfer real property in Anaheim using a quitclaim deed, the grantor must sign before a notary public, then record the deed with the Orange County recorder of deeds. The transfer is legally effective against third parties only after recording.

Quitclaim deeds in Anaheim are commonly used for transfers between spouses (divorce or marriage), parent-to-child transfers, transfers to a revocable living trust, or transferring a personal property into an LLC. For arm's-length sales, use a warranty deed instead.

FAQ

Anaheim quitclaim deed questions

Where do I record a quitclaim deed in Anaheim?
Record the deed at the Orange County recorder or register of deeds office. Bring the original notarized deed plus the recording fee. Some counties accept mail-in recording. The deed becomes public record once filed.
How much does it cost to record a deed in Anaheim?
Recording fees in Orange County vary but are typically $10–$60 per page. Many states also impose a documentary transfer or conveyance tax based on the sale price. See the California quitclaim deed state page for the current transfer tax rate.
Do I need a lawyer to prepare a quitclaim deed in Anaheim?
A lawyer is not legally required to prepare a quitclaim deed in Anaheim for simple transfers (spouses, family, to a trust). However, for complex situations — transfers affecting a mortgage, transfers with tax implications, or disputed ownership — consulting a California real estate attorney is recommended.
How long does recording a deed take in Anaheim?
Recording is typically same-day or within a few business days when you bring the deed to the Orange County recorder's office in person. Mail-in recording may take 1–2 weeks. You will receive a stamped copy with the recording information as proof of recording.
