How to Register an LLC: Step-by-Step (2026)

Forming an LLC takes about 1–2 hours online and costs $50–$500 depending on your state. Here's the exact sequence.

Step 1: Choose Your State

Most businesses should form their LLC in the state where they operate. The myth of Delaware or Wyoming for small businesses: yes, those states have favorable laws for large corporations — but a small LLC operating in Texas that forms in Delaware must still register as a "foreign LLC" in Texas and pay Texas filing fees. You end up paying both states. Form in your home state unless you have specific reasons not to.

Exception: if you have significant outside investors, plan to raise venture capital, or will be issuing complex equity structures, Delaware's legal predictability (Court of Chancery, well-established case law) may be worth it.

Step 2: Name Your LLC

Your LLC name must: (1) Include "LLC," "L.L.C.," "Limited Liability Company," or an accepted abbreviation. (2) Be distinguishable from existing business names in your state (check the Secretary of State's online name database). (3) Not use restricted words (Bank, Insurance, Attorney, etc.) without special approval.

Reserve the name if your state offers it ($10–$50 for 30–120 days) while you prepare your filing. Also secure the domain name and social media handles before filing — the name is worthless if the domain is taken.

Step 3: File Articles of Organization

File with your state's Secretary of State (or equivalent — in some states it's the Department of State or Division of Corporations). Most states now accept online filing. You'll need: LLC name, registered agent name and address (a person or company that receives legal mail on behalf of the LLC), the principal business address, and the names of members or managers.

Filing fees: Wyoming $100, Colorado $50, Florida $125, Texas $300, California $70 (but then $800/yr minimum franchise tax), New York $200 (but then a $1,000+ publication requirement). Your articles are approved instantly or within 1–10 business days depending on state.

Step 4: EIN, Bank Account, and Operating Agreement

EIN: Apply for an Employer Identification Number from the IRS at irs.gov — it's free and instant. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file business taxes. Even single-member LLCs should get an EIN to avoid using their Social Security number on vendor forms.

Bank account: Never mix personal and business funds. Open a dedicated business checking account using your LLC's name, articles of organization, and EIN. Commingling is the #1 reason courts pierce the corporate veil. Operating agreement: Draft and sign an operating agreement even if your state doesn't require one — it proves the LLC is a separate, legitimate entity.

FAQ

Can I form an LLC without a lawyer?
Yes. Online LLC formation is straightforward in most states. For a standard single-member or simple two-member LLC, DIY filing with a well-drafted operating agreement is sufficient. For complex multi-member LLCs, family business structures, or LLCs with outside investment, an attorney's guidance on the operating agreement is worth the cost.
How long does LLC formation take?
Online filing approval times: Wyoming and Delaware — 24 hours; Florida, Colorado, Texas — 1–5 business days; California — 5–10 business days; New York — 1–2 weeks. Expedited filing for a higher fee is available in most states (Delaware can be same-day).
What is a registered agent?
A registered agent is a person or company with a physical street address in the LLC's state who receives official legal documents (lawsuits, state notices) on behalf of the LLC during business hours. You can be your own registered agent if you have a physical address (not a PO box) in the state. Registered agent services cost $50–$150/year.
Do I need a business license in addition to an LLC?
Yes. An LLC is a legal structure, not a business license. You still need applicable local business licenses, professional licenses, sales tax permits, and industry-specific permits to operate legally. Check your city, county, and state requirements — these are separate from the LLC formation filing.
