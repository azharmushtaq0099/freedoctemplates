How to Write a Notice to Vacate as a Tenant (2026)

Leaving a rental without proper written notice can cost you one to two months' rent in lease-break penalties — even if you're month-to-month.

When and How Much Notice Is Required

The notice period you must give depends on: (1) your lease agreement (always check first — it may require 60 days even for month-to-month), (2) your state's law (default is usually 30 days for month-to-month; your state may differ), and (3) whether you are ending a fixed-term lease early (different rules apply).

State-specific notice rules for tenants giving notice: California — 30 days (1 year or less tenancy); California — 60 days (over 1 year); Texas — same as rent payment period (monthly = 30 days); New York — 30 days for month-to-month; Florida — 15 days for month-to-month (or one rental period). Always verify your state's current rule.

What the Notice Letter Must Say

Your notice to vacate must include: (1) Your full name and the names of all tenants on the lease. (2) The rental property address. (3) The date you are giving the notice. (4) The date you intend to vacate (at least the minimum notice period from today). (5) A statement that you are terminating the tenancy. (6) Your forwarding address for the security deposit return. (7) Your signature.

Keep it professional and neutral — no need to explain why you're leaving. "I am writing to give [X] days' notice of my intention to vacate the property at [address] on [date]" is a complete notice.

How to Deliver It

Certified mail with return receipt is the gold standard — you get signed proof of delivery. Email is acceptable in many states (check your lease for email notice provisions) and creates a timestamp. Hand delivery with the landlord's signature acknowledging receipt also works.

Never assume verbal notice counts. "I told my landlord I was leaving" will not protect you in court. Written, documented notice is the only notice that matters.

Breaking a Fixed-Term Lease Early

A different process applies if you are leaving before a fixed-term lease ends. You generally owe rent through the end of the lease term OR until the landlord re-rents the unit (most states require landlords to "mitigate damages" by actively looking for a new tenant). You may also owe a lease-break fee if your lease specifies one.

Special situations that may allow early termination without penalty: domestic violence (most states have laws allowing immediate termination for DV survivors), military deployment (SCRA allows service members to break leases with 30 days notice), landlord breach of habitability (if the unit is uninhabitable and the landlord fails to repair after notice), or constructive eviction (landlord's actions make the unit unlivable).

FAQ

Can a landlord keep the security deposit if I give proper notice?
Not for giving notice alone. If you give proper notice, leave the unit clean and undamaged (beyond normal wear and tear), and return all keys, the landlord must return the full deposit within the statutory deadline (14–30 days in most states). They can only deduct for legitimate damages or unpaid rent.
What if I need to leave immediately (domestic violence, habitability emergency)?
Most states have emergency early termination rights: (1) Domestic violence — every state has laws allowing immediate or very short notice lease termination for DV victims, usually requiring a police report or restraining order. (2) Habitability — if the unit is genuinely uninhabitable (no heat, sewage issues, mold) and the landlord has failed to repair after written notice, many states allow lease termination without penalty.
Does my notice to vacate need to be notarized?
No. A notice to vacate does not need to be notarized to be valid. It must be in writing, signed, and properly delivered. The goal is a documented paper trail — notarization adds nothing for this purpose.
What if the landlord claims they never received my notice?
This is why delivery documentation matters. With certified mail, you have the return receipt. With email, you have the sent and read receipt. If you hand-delivered, have a witness or ask the landlord to sign a copy. "I didn't receive it" is almost impossible to argue against a green return receipt card.
