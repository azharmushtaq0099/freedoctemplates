California Power of Attorney — Execution Requirements

To be legally valid in California, a power of attorney must be executed according to California state law:

  • Witnesses: 2 witnesses OR notarization
  • Notarization: Required (or 2 witnesses)

California POA is governed by Probate Code Sections 4000–4465. A durable POA must include specific durability language. Healthcare POA (AHCD) requires either 2 witnesses or notarization, and specific witnesses (not care providers, not heirs) are required.

After completing this form, sign it before a California notary public. Keep the original in a safe place and give copies to the agent, your bank, and any relevant healthcare providers.

FAQ

California Power of Attorney questions

What are the signing requirements for a California power of attorney?
In California, a valid POA requires: 2 witnesses OR notarization and Required (or 2 witnesses). California POA is governed by Probate Code Sections 4000–4465. A durable POA must include specific durability language. Healthcare POA (AHCD) requires either 2 witnesses or notarization, and specific witnesses (not care providers, not heirs) are required.
Does a California power of attorney need to be filed with the court?
No — a California power of attorney is an internal document. You do not file it with any court or government agency unless it involves a real estate transaction, in which case it may need to be recorded with the county recorder.
Can I use a California POA in another state?
Generally yes. Most states honor a validly executed out-of-state POA, especially if it meets the more demanding state's requirements. If you need to use a California POA for real estate in another state, consult an attorney about that state's acceptance standards.
How do I revoke a California power of attorney?
To revoke a California POA: sign a written Notice of Revocation before a notary, deliver it to the agent, and notify any institutions (banks, healthcare providers) that relied on the POA. You should also collect and destroy existing copies of the original POA.
