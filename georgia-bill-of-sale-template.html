Georgia Bill of Sale — Key Requirements

In Georgia, a bill of sale documents the private sale of a vehicle or personal property. Key Georgia-specific rules:

  • Notary: Not required.
  • Odometer: Required for vehicles under 10 years old.
  • Title fee: $18 at the DMV/county clerk.

Georgia requires a completed Bill of Sale (Form T-7) for tag/title transfer. Emissions inspection may be required in certain counties (Atlanta metro). Title transfer within 30 days.

FAQ

Georgia Bill of Sale questions

Does a Georgia bill of sale need to be notarized?
In Georgia: Not required. Georgia requires a completed Bill of Sale (Form T-7) for tag/title transfer. Emissions inspection may be required in certain counties (Atlanta metro). Title transfer within 30 days.
How do I transfer a vehicle title in Georgia?
After completing the bill of sale, both parties sign the title. Take the signed title, bill of sale, and payment for the title transfer fee ($18) to the Georgia DMV or county clerk. The buyer typically has 30 days to complete the transfer.
What should a Georgia bill of sale include?
A valid Georgia bill of sale should include: seller and buyer full names and addresses, vehicle description (year, make, model, VIN), sale price and date, odometer reading (for vehicles under 10 years old), and signatures of both parties.
Do I need a bill of sale for a private vehicle sale in Georgia?
Yes. While Georgia may not always legally require a separate bill of sale (some states just use the title), a bill of sale protects both buyer and seller by documenting the transaction details, "as-is" condition, and agreed price. Georgia requires a bill of sale or equivalent documentation for title transfer.
