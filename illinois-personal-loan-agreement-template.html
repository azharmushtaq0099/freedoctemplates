Personal Loans in Illinois

A personal loan agreement in Illinois documents a loan between two individuals — commonly between family members, friends, or colleagues. The maximum interest rate allowed is 9% general / higher by contract. Charging above this limit may cause the interest (or the entire note) to be unenforceable.

For loans of $10,000 or more between family members, the IRS requires a minimum interest rate (the Applicable Federal Rate, or AFR) to avoid characterizing the loan as a gift. Always document family loans with a signed written agreement and keep records of all payments.

FAQ

Illinois personal loan questions

What is the maximum interest rate for a personal loan in Illinois?
The maximum interest rate for a personal loan in Illinois is 9% general / higher by contract. Charging above this rate may result in the lender forfeiting all interest or facing other penalties under Illinois usury law. Always state the interest rate in writing.
Does a personal loan agreement need to be notarized in Illinois?
A personal loan agreement in Illinois does not legally need to be notarized to be enforceable. Both parties should sign the agreement, with each keeping a copy. Notarization adds credibility and is recommended for larger loan amounts.
What happens if someone doesn't repay a personal loan in Illinois?
If the borrower defaults, the lender should send a written demand for payment first. If the borrower still does not pay, the lender can sue in Illinois small claims court (for smaller amounts) or civil court. The signed loan agreement is the primary evidence of the debt and the repayment terms.
Are loans between family members enforceable in Illinois?
Yes. Loans between family members in Illinois are legally enforceable if documented in a signed written agreement. Without documentation, it can be difficult to distinguish a loan from a gift if a dispute arises. A written agreement with a repayment schedule protects both parties.
