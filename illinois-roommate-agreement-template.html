Roommate Agreements in Illinois

A roommate agreement in Illinois is a private contract between co-tenants that governs how the shared space will be managed. It covers rent splits, utility shares, cleaning duties, guest policies, quiet hours, and what happens if a roommate needs to leave early.

A roommate agreement is separate from the lease — it does not change who is responsible to the landlord. If all roommates are on the master lease, all are jointly liable for the full rent. The roommate agreement governs the relationship between the tenants.

FAQ

Illinois roommate agreement questions

Is a roommate agreement legally enforceable in Illinois?
Yes. A signed roommate agreement in Illinois is an enforceable contract between co-tenants. If a roommate violates the agreement — for example, by not paying their share of rent — the other roommates can pursue legal action to recover the unpaid amount in small claims court.
Does a roommate agreement override the master lease in Illinois?
No. The master lease between the tenant(s) and the landlord takes priority. A roommate agreement governs the relationship between co-tenants, not the relationship with the landlord. All tenants on the master lease remain jointly and individually responsible for the full rent.
Can I add a new roommate in Illinois without telling the landlord?
Generally no. In Illinois, adding a new occupant or subletting typically requires landlord consent. Check your lease agreement for specific subletting provisions. Violating the lease by adding an unauthorized occupant could result in an eviction notice.
What should a roommate agreement include?
A good roommate agreement should include: (1) each roommate's rent share, (2) how utilities are split, (3) a cleaning schedule, (4) guest and overnight visitor policies, (5) quiet hours, (6) what happens if a roommate wants to leave before the lease ends, and (7) how the security deposit will be divided when you move out.
