Power of Attorney in Las Vegas, NV

A power of attorney (POA) in Las Vegas is governed by Nevada law. The principal (person granting authority) must sign the POA before a notary public. Most Nevada POAs also require one or two witnesses, depending on the type of POA.

In Las Vegas, notary services are widely available at banks (Bank of America, Chase, Wells Fargo), shipping stores (UPS, FedEx), title companies, law offices, and online through remote notarization services. A durable POA remains in effect even if the principal becomes incapacitated.

FAQ

Las Vegas power of attorney questions

Where can I get a power of attorney notarized in Las Vegas?
In Las Vegas, POA notarization services are available at banks (free for account holders), UPS and FedEx stores, title companies, law offices, hospitals (for medical POAs), and through online notary services (if Nevada permits remote notarization). Mobile notaries will also come to your location.
How many witnesses does a POA require in Nevada?
Witness requirements vary by Nevada law and the type of POA. Financial (general/durable) POAs typically require 1–2 witnesses plus a notary. Healthcare POAs often require 2 witnesses. Check the Nevada power of attorney state page for current witness requirements.
What is the difference between a durable and a limited POA?
A durable POA remains in effect if the principal becomes incapacitated — it is used for long-term financial planning and elder care. A limited POA authorizes the agent to perform a specific act (like signing a real estate closing) and expires after that act is complete.
Does a power of attorney expire in Nevada?
A durable POA in Nevada does not expire unless you set an expiration date in the document or revoke it in writing. It automatically terminates at the principal's death. A non-durable POA automatically terminates if the principal becomes incapacitated.
