FAQ

LLC questions answered

What states require an LLC operating agreement?
Five states legally require LLCs to have an operating agreement: California, Delaware, Maine, Missouri, and New York. Even in states that do not require one, an operating agreement is strongly recommended because without one, your state's default LLC rules govern your business, which may not match your intentions.
What is the difference between member-managed and manager-managed?
In a member-managed LLC, all members (owners) participate in day-to-day decisions. This is the most common structure for small LLCs. In a manager-managed LLC, a designated manager (who may or may not be a member) handles operations, while passive members invest but do not manage. Manager-managed LLCs are common when some members are investors.
Do I file the operating agreement with the state?
No. The operating agreement is an internal document — you keep it in your business records. You do not file it with the state (unlike the Articles of Organization, which is the formation document you file with your state's business registry). Your bank may ask to see it when opening a business account.
Can I amend an operating agreement after signing?
Yes. Operating agreements can be amended by member vote according to the amendment procedures in the document. Typically, all members must agree to an amendment in writing. Keep all signed versions of your operating agreement for your records.
