Lease Agreements in Milwaukee, WI

Residential leases in Milwaukee are governed by Wisconsin landlord-tenant law. Rent control status in Milwaukee: No. Both landlords and tenants should have a signed written lease agreement before any tenancy begins — it defines rent, deposit, maintenance responsibilities, and the notice required to end the tenancy.

If a lease dispute goes to court in Milwaukee, it is filed at Milwaukee County Circuit Court. A clear, state-compliant lease agreement is your best protection against disputes.

FAQ

Milwaukee lease questions

Is there rent control in Milwaukee?
Rent control status in Milwaukee: No. Check whether your specific property is covered — exemptions commonly apply to newer construction, single-family homes, or owner-occupied duplexes. Consult a local attorney if unsure.
What court handles evictions in Milwaukee?
Eviction cases in Milwaukee are filed at the Milwaukee County Circuit Court. After serving the required notice (typically 3–14 days depending on the violation), the landlord files an unlawful detainer or eviction lawsuit in this court.
Does a lease in Milwaukee need to be notarized?
No. A residential lease agreement in Milwaukee does not need to be notarized to be legally binding. It is enforceable once both the landlord and tenant sign it. Both parties should keep a signed copy.
What is the security deposit limit in Milwaukee?
Security deposit limits are set by Wisconsin state law, not by the city. Check the Wisconsin lease agreement state page for the current security deposit limit, interest requirements, and return deadline.
