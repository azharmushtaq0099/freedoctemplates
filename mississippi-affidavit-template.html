Affidavits in Mississippi

An affidavit in Mississippi is a written statement of facts sworn under oath before a notary public. The person signing (the affiant) must personally appear before a licensed Mississippi notary, swear that the statements are true, and sign the document in the notary's presence.

Common uses include: general statements of fact for court proceedings, affidavits of heirship (to transfer property without probate), financial affidavits (for divorce or support proceedings), affidavits of residency, and affidavits of service.

FAQ

Mississippi affidavit questions

Does an affidavit need to be notarized in Mississippi?
Yes. An affidavit in Mississippi must be signed in the presence of a licensed notary public who administers an oath. Without notarization, the document is simply a written statement, not a sworn affidavit. Courts in Mississippi require notarized affidavits for most filings.
Can I use an affidavit of heirship in Mississippi?
Yes. An affidavit of heirship is commonly used in Mississippi to transfer real property without going through the full probate process, especially when the deceased did not leave a will. It must be signed by two disinterested witnesses who knew the deceased and notarized. Check with the county recorder for specific requirements.
What is the difference between an affidavit and a declaration?
An affidavit is sworn before a notary under oath; making a false statement is perjury. A declaration (used under 28 U.S.C. § 1746) is signed under penalty of perjury but does not require a notary. Federal courts generally accept both; Mississippi courts may prefer notarized affidavits for state filings.
Where can I get an affidavit notarized in Mississippi?
In Mississippi, you can get an affidavit notarized at banks, UPS/FedEx stores, law offices, title companies, courthouses, and through online notary services (if remote notarization is permitted in Mississippi). Mobile notaries are also available who will come to your location.
