How to Make a Valid Will in Montana

Montana Will Execution Requirements

To create a legally valid will in Montana, the testator must be at least 18 years old and of sound mind. The will must be in writing, signed by the testator, and witnessed by 2 competent witnesses who are present at the time of signing and are not beneficiaries named in the will.

Montana recognizes holographic wills — wills written entirely in the testator's own handwriting, with no witnesses required. A formally witnessed will is still recommended as it is easier to probate and harder to contest.

A self-proving affidavit is available in Montana — notarize the will at signing so witnesses do not need to testify in probate court, saving time and cost.

FAQ

Montana will questions

How many witnesses does a will require in Montana?
Montana requires 2 witnesses to sign the will in the testator's presence. Witnesses must be adults who are not named as beneficiaries. All parties should sign at the same ceremony.
Does Montana recognize a holographic (handwritten) will?
Yes. Montana recognizes holographic wills written entirely in the testator's own handwriting. No witnesses are required for a holographic will in Montana, but a formally witnessed will is recommended for easier probate.
Does a will in Montana need to be notarized?
Notarization is not required to make a will valid in Montana, but a notarized self-proving affidavit is highly recommended. It means witnesses do not need to testify at probate, saving time and cost.
What happens if you die without a will in Montana?
Dying without a will in Montana means your estate passes under intestacy laws — generally to a surviving spouse first, then to children, then to more distant relatives. You cannot name a guardian for minor children or direct specific assets to specific people without a valid will.
