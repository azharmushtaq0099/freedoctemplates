Notice to Vacate in Nevada

A notice to vacate in Nevada is a written notice from either the tenant or the landlord to end a rental tenancy. For month-to-month rentals, tenants in Nevada generally need to give 30 days written notice before their intended move-out date. Send via certified mail to create proof of delivery.

This is different from an eviction notice — a notice to vacate is used when the tenant or landlord simply wants to end the tenancy, not due to a lease violation. If you are ending a fixed-term lease, check your lease agreement for any specific notice requirements.

FAQ

Nevada notice to vacate questions

How much notice does a tenant need to give in Nevada?
For a month-to-month tenancy in Nevada, a tenant must give 30 days' written notice before the intended move-out date. If your rental agreement specifies a different notice period, follow the lease terms.
What is the difference between a notice to vacate and an eviction notice in Nevada?
A notice to vacate is sent when either party wants to end the tenancy — there is no lease violation. An eviction notice (pay or quit, cure or quit) is served because the tenant violated the lease. A notice to vacate can come from the tenant (I'm moving out) or the landlord (please leave by this date).
Does a notice to vacate in Nevada need to be delivered by certified mail?
Certified mail is not always legally required in Nevada, but it is strongly recommended. A certified mail receipt proves the date and fact of delivery, which is important if there is a dispute about when notice was given or whether it was received.
What happens if a tenant does not vacate after a notice in Nevada?
If the tenant fails to vacate after the notice period expires in Nevada, the landlord must file for formal eviction (unlawful detainer) in court. The landlord cannot lock the tenant out, remove belongings, or cut utilities — these are illegal self-help evictions in all states.
