Ohio Power of Attorney — Signing Requirements

To be legally valid in Ohio, a power of attorney must comply with Ohio state law:

  • Witnesses: No witnesses required
  • Notarization: Required

Ohio adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act (R.C. 1337.21). Notarization required. Ohio courts presume good faith for agents acting under a POA. Financial institutions must accept within reasonable time.

After completing this form, sign it before a notary public with the required witnesses present. Keep the original in a secure location and give copies to your agent, bank, and healthcare providers.

FAQ

Ohio Power of Attorney questions

What are the signing requirements for a Ohio power of attorney?
In Ohio, a valid POA requires: No witnesses required and Required. Ohio adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act (R.C. 1337.21). Notarization required. Ohio courts presume good faith for agents acting under a POA. Financial institutions must accept within reasonable time.
Does a Ohio power of attorney need to be filed with the court?
No. A Ohio power of attorney is an internal document — you keep it in your records. You do not file it with any court. For real estate transactions, the POA may need to be recorded with the county recorder.
Can I use a Ohio POA in another state?
Generally yes. Most states honor a validly executed out-of-state POA, especially one that meets the more demanding state's requirements. If the POA involves real estate in another state, consult an attorney about that state's acceptance standards.
How do I revoke a Ohio power of attorney?
To revoke a Ohio POA: sign a written Revocation of Power of Attorney before a notary, deliver it to the agent in writing, and notify all institutions that relied on the POA. Collect and destroy existing copies of the original where possible.
