How to Record a Quitclaim Deed in Oregon

Oregon Quitclaim Deed Recording Process

To transfer property using a quitclaim deed in Oregon, the grantor must sign the deed before a notary public. The deed is then recorded with the county recorder (or register of deeds) in the county where the property is located. Recording fees in Oregon are approximately $60/page.

A documentary transfer tax of $1–$2/$1,000 (county) applies in Oregon. Family transfers and certain exempt transfers may qualify for an exclusion — consult a real estate attorney to confirm eligibility.

FAQ

Oregon quitclaim deed questions

Does a quitclaim deed in Oregon need to be notarized?
Yes. A quitclaim deed in Oregon must be signed in front of a notary public before it can be recorded. Without notarization, the county recorder will not accept the deed for recording, and the transfer will not be legally effective against third parties.
Where do I record a quitclaim deed in Oregon?
Record the deed with the county recorder or register of deeds in the county where the property is located. Bring the original notarized deed plus the recording fee (approximately $60/page). Some counties accept mail-in recording.
What is the transfer tax on a quitclaim deed in Oregon?
The transfer tax in Oregon is $1–$2/$1,000 (county). Certain transfers (spouses, parent-child, to a trust) may be exempt. Check with the county assessor or a real estate attorney before recording.
How long does a quitclaim deed take in Oregon?
Once you bring the notarized deed to the county recorder's office in Oregon, recording is typically same-day or within a few business days. The transfer is legally effective against third parties from the date of recording.
