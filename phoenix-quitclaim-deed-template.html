Recording a Quitclaim Deed in Phoenix

To transfer real property in Phoenix using a quitclaim deed, the grantor must sign before a notary public, then record the deed with the Maricopa County recorder of deeds. The transfer is legally effective against third parties only after recording.

Quitclaim deeds in Phoenix are commonly used for transfers between spouses (divorce or marriage), parent-to-child transfers, transfers to a revocable living trust, or transferring a personal property into an LLC. For arm's-length sales, use a warranty deed instead.

FAQ

Phoenix quitclaim deed questions

Where do I record a quitclaim deed in Phoenix?
Record the deed at the Maricopa County recorder or register of deeds office. Bring the original notarized deed plus the recording fee. Some counties accept mail-in recording. The deed becomes public record once filed.
How much does it cost to record a deed in Phoenix?
Recording fees in Maricopa County vary but are typically $10–$60 per page. Many states also impose a documentary transfer or conveyance tax based on the sale price. See the Arizona quitclaim deed state page for the current transfer tax rate.
Do I need a lawyer to prepare a quitclaim deed in Phoenix?
A lawyer is not legally required to prepare a quitclaim deed in Phoenix for simple transfers (spouses, family, to a trust). However, for complex situations — transfers affecting a mortgage, transfers with tax implications, or disputed ownership — consulting a Arizona real estate attorney is recommended.
How long does recording a deed take in Phoenix?
Recording is typically same-day or within a few business days when you bring the deed to the Maricopa County recorder's office in person. Mail-in recording may take 1–2 weeks. You will receive a stamped copy with the recording information as proof of recording.
