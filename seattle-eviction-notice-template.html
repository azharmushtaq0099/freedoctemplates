Eviction Process in Seattle, WA

Evictions in Seattle are governed by Washington landlord-tenant law. The landlord must first serve a written notice (pay or quit, cure or quit, or unconditional quit) and wait for the notice period to expire before filing at the King County District Court.

Self-help evictions — changing locks, removing belongings, or cutting utilities without a court order — are illegal everywhere, including Seattle. Always follow the formal notice and court process.

FAQ

Seattle eviction questions

Where do I file an eviction in Seattle?
Eviction (unlawful detainer) cases in Seattle are filed at the King County District Court. After the notice period expires without the tenant paying or leaving, the landlord files an eviction petition and the court schedules a hearing.
How long does eviction take in Seattle?
The eviction process in Seattle typically takes 4–10 weeks from serving notice to the sheriff enforcing a writ of possession. This includes the notice period, court filing, a hearing date (usually 1–3 weeks out), and if the landlord wins, the time to obtain and execute the writ.
Does Seattle have just cause eviction requirements?
Just cause eviction requirements in Seattle: No (state ban). Where just cause protections apply, landlords cannot evict tenants without a legally recognized reason (non-payment, lease violation, owner move-in, etc.) even if the lease has ended.
Can a landlord evict a tenant without notice in Seattle?
No. Seattle landlords must serve proper written notice and wait for the notice period to expire before filing for eviction. Locking out a tenant, removing their belongings, or cutting utilities without a court order is an illegal self-help eviction.
