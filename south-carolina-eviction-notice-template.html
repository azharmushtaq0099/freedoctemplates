South Carolina Eviction Process

How to Serve an Eviction Notice in South Carolina

In South Carolina, landlords must provide written notice before filing for eviction. For non-payment of rent, give the tenant 5 days to pay or vacate. For lease violations, the tenant has 14 days to cure the violation or leave. To terminate a month-to-month tenancy without cause, 30 days' notice is required.

After the notice period expires without compliance, the landlord may file for eviction in South Carolina court. Changing locks, removing belongings, or cutting utilities to force a tenant out are illegal self-help evictions in all states.

FAQ

South Carolina eviction questions

How many days notice is required for non-payment of rent in South Carolina?
South Carolina landlords must give tenants 5 days to pay the overdue rent or vacate before filing for eviction. The notice must be in writing and delivered per South Carolina's service requirements.
How long does the eviction process take in South Carolina?
After the 5 days notice expires unpaid, the landlord files an eviction lawsuit. Court hearings are typically set within 1–3 weeks. If the landlord wins, a writ of possession is issued and the sheriff enforces the eviction. The full process usually takes 3–8 weeks depending on court schedule and any tenant appeal.
Can a landlord evict without notice in South Carolina?
No. South Carolina landlords must provide written notice and wait for the period to expire before filing for eviction. Changing locks, removing belongings, or shutting off utilities without a court order are illegal self-help evictions in South Carolina.
What notice is needed to end a month-to-month lease in South Carolina?
To terminate a month-to-month tenancy in South Carolina without cause, the landlord must provide 30 days' written notice. The notice should state the date by which the tenant must vacate.
