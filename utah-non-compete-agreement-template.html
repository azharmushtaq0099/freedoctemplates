Non-Compete Agreements in Utah

Non-compete agreements in Utah are limited when they protect a legitimate business interest and are reasonable in scope. The agreement must specify a geographic area, the type of restricted activity, and a time limit of no more than 1 year to be enforceable.

FAQ

Utah non-compete questions

Are non-compete agreements enforceable in Utah?
Utah enforces non-compete agreements that are reasonable in time (generally up to 1 year), geographic area, and scope of restricted activities. Courts may modify overbroad terms.
How long can a non-compete last in Utah?
Courts in Utah generally enforce non-competes for up to 1 year. Longer periods are more likely to be modified or rejected by courts. The shorter and more targeted the restriction, the more likely it is to be enforced.
Can an employee be fired for violating a non-compete in Utah?
Yes. If an employee violates a valid non-compete in Utah, the employer can seek an injunction to stop the violation and may sue for damages. The employee can also be terminated for breach of contract.
Does a non-compete need to be notarized in Utah?
No. A non-compete agreement in Utah does not need to be notarized to be enforceable. Both parties must sign the agreement, which should be provided to the employee before or at the time of hire, along with a copy of what they are signing.
