Vermont NDA Law

Enforcing an NDA in Vermont

Non-disclosure agreements in Vermont are governed by state contract law and the VUTSA trade secret statute. To be enforceable, an NDA must protect a legitimate business interest (such as trade secrets, customer lists, or proprietary processes), have a reasonable duration, and be supported by adequate consideration.

Employee NDAs are generally enforceable in Vermont when they have reasonable scope. Courts in Vermont may narrow overbroad NDA terms rather than voiding the entire agreement.

FAQ

Vermont NDA questions

Is an NDA enforceable in Vermont?
Yes — NDAs with reasonable scope, duration, and consideration are enforceable in Vermont under VUTSA. Courts may narrow overbroad terms rather than striking the whole agreement. Both parties must sign for the agreement to be binding.
What trade secret law applies in Vermont?
Vermont follows the VUTSA for state trade secret claims. Federal trade secret claims are governed by the Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA, 2016). Most NDAs should specify that both statutes may apply.
How long can an NDA last in Vermont?
NDA duration in Vermont should be tied to the type of information protected. For trade secrets that remain confidential, indefinite or long-term NDAs are more acceptable. For general business information, 2–5 years is typical. An "indefinite" NDA for general business information is more likely to be challenged in court.
Does an NDA need to be notarized in Vermont?
No. An NDA in Vermont does not need to be notarized to be enforceable. Both parties must simply sign the agreement. Keeping a signed copy with each party is the best practice for recordkeeping.
